## Raw and minimally processed foods

Eating raw or undercooked meat, organs, fish, shellfish, eggs or unpasteurised dairy carries a real risk of foodborne illness from bacteria, viruses, parasites and prions. These illnesses can be severe, cause lasting harm or be fatal. Freezing, ageing, curing or sourcing from a trusted supplier reduces some risks but does not eliminate them. The risk is higher for children, older adults, people who are pregnant or breastfeeding, and anyone with a weakened immune system or chronic illness, and these groups should not eat raw animal foods.

A low risk score, a "safe" status, a food recall check or any other result in the App is never a guarantee that food is safe to eat. We do not inspect, test or verify any food, supplier, farm, batch or storage practice, and recall information may be incomplete or delayed.

## Diets, fasting and supplements

Restrictive diets such as carnivore, animal-based and raw diets, and fasting, may not be appropriate for everyone and can affect conditions such as diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease, gout, eating disorders and pregnancy, as well as how medication works. Talk to your doctor before trying them.

## Sun exposure

Sunlight raises the risk of sunburn, skin damage and skin cancer. Sunlight and vitamin D figures in the App are rough estimates based on location, time and weather, not measurements of your skin or blood. Avoid burning, and follow your doctor's advice about sun exposure and vitamin D.

## Estimates may be wrong

Nutrition data comes from public databases, product labels, your own entries and artificial-intelligence estimates of photos, and may be incomplete or inaccurate. Water from food, portion sizes, targets, scores and trends are approximations. Check anything important against the product label or a professional.

## Who should talk to a doctor first

Consult a qualified healthcare professional before using the App to guide your diet if you are pregnant, trying to become pregnant or breastfeeding; are under 18; are over 65; have, or have had, an eating disorder; have a chronic illness, a weakened immune system, or a kidney, liver, heart or metabolic condition; or take prescription medication.
